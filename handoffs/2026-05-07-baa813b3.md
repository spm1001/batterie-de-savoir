### Reflection

Three things from this session worth carrying forward:

**1. Hypothesis-as-finding fired again, with a sharper edge.** The "rewrite-on-update with silent failure-by-omission" theory had structured supporting evidence (identical-to-the-millisecond timestamps across 5 entries) and felt mechanically coherent. I led with it confidently in user-facing text, with only soft hedging ("I can't prove without a fresh repro"). The diff disproved it instantly. Logged in self.md as the latest incident in the hypothesis-as-finding cluster. The discipline upgrade I named: when a theory has multiple supporting datapoints AND feels coherent, *that* is the moment to design the disproving experiment, not the moment to write up the theory more carefully. The ritual that broke it open was "let's snapshot and run it" — running the experiment, not refining the theory.

**2. Handoffs encode a moment, not a priority.** I read the May 3 Codex-setup handoff and treated the Codex skill bridge as ongoing infrastructure that needs maintenance — kept dragging it into recommendations throughout the session ("if /plugins reinstalls fresh and the version bumps, Codex symlinks may go stale — that's literally what bon-mohala is about"). Sameer caught it cleanly: "where are *you* seeing that? it was just so I could use Codex but piggybacked on the same Claude structures that are my primary." Codex isn't load-bearing; the May 3 work was a side experiment. The handoff format gives no priority signal — exploratory and load-bearing read identically. Logged in self.md as a new top-level entry. Implications: when reading a handoff, ask "is this the user's primary stack?" before treating its concerns as live; when writing one for exploratory work, say so explicitly.

**3. Compounding effect of overstated handoffs.** The May 3 handoff said "Filed follow-up bons: bon-mohala, bon-kapuko" — neither bon exists in any repo. So I read the handoff confidently believing those were tracked, and propagated that belief forward through the session. When the form invites confidence, it gets confidence whether or not the substance warrants it. This pairs naturally with point 2 — both reflect ways the handoff format can mislead the next Claude when discipline lapses.

## For Claudes to come

When you read a handoff, two questions to ask before treating its concerns as live: (1) Is this work the user's primary stack, or a side experiment? The handoff format gives no priority signal — a one-day setup project reads identically to load-bearing infrastructure. (2) Were the follow-up bons actually filed? Don't trust "Filed follow-up bons: X, Y" without checking. Both failures compound: overstatement creates false confidence in the next Claude, and unmarked exploratory work gets treated as live concern. If you're writing a handoff for work that's exploratory, say so explicitly — "this was a side experiment, not part of the daily workflow." Future-you will weigh it accordingly.
